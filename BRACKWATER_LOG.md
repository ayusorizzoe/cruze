# BRACKWATER_LOG

Living record of narrative development, artistic decisions, worldbuilding, and cross-site relationships for **Brackwater**.

**Status labels:** `established` (explicitly confirmed by the artist or present in the files) · `provisional` (proposed or being explored) · `unresolved` (unknown, ambiguous, or undetermined) · `superseded` (deliberately revised).
**Information types:** **[published]** = readers can encounter it on a site · **[development]** = in code comments, data placeholders, or artist instruction, but not visible as content · **[interpretation]** = what an element may be doing artistically. Interpretations are not canon.

Paths are relative to the repo root. Unless noted otherwise, everything here comes from the files as of 2026-11-11.

---

## A. Project overview

Brackwater is an experimental work of trans fiction for trans and nonbinary readers. It builds a fictional coastal town out of interconnected websites. The websites themselves are the medium: their structures, institutional voices, omissions, navigation, and relationships to each other carry the storytelling. There is no protagonist, no prescribed reading order, and no central mystery. The aim is recognition rather than explanation. The gender binary is treated as a dominant inherited myth, hardship and joy share the same lives, and inherited conditions (infrastructure, institutions, environment) shape what people can do. *(Source: artist instructions, 2026-11-11.)*

**Repo name vs. project name.** The repository is called `cruze`, after the in-fiction search engine/browser that frames every site. "Brackwater" is the name of the town and of the whole work.

| Functionality | **Search (2026-11-14):** each site is matched by name, subjects (`keywords` in the sites list), description and the text of its pages. Page text is fetched once per page view when someone starts searching; Salome's text comes from `salomeData.js`. Matching is by word prefix and ignores filler words (e.g. "the", "where"). Results are ranked name > subject > description > page text, and weaker matches are dropped. Suggestions update while typing. **Enter opens the results page.** An empty field shows nothing. **No match:** "No matching results found." then "Other websites to explore" with 3 sites picked at random once per page view, never presented as answers. If page text can't load (e.g. opened from `file://`), names, subjects and descriptions still match. Visited sites are marked purple (`sessionStorage`, current tab only, never sent anywhere). No analytics, accounts, cookies or stored search history. |
| Visual identity | Search-engine minimalism: white, Atkinson Hyperlegible, grey pill input. The **only** animation is a short pink/blue (trans flag colours) glitch on the Cruze logo, every 45–90 seconds at random, turned off for reduced-motion visitors. The tagline and all text and button glitches were removed (2026-11-14). |
| Voice | **Two registers.** About and How Search Works stay in Cruze's fictional institutional voice (a small regional team, "our index is still growing"). Help and Privacy openly say that Brackwater is a fictional, single creative project. Advertising is a real invitation to trans and nonbinary people to contribute work to the town: authors keep ownership, permission and credit, and can ask for removal. Submissions aren't open yet, and there's no form or address. |

## E. Locations and institutions

- **Brackwater (town):** low-lying coastal town of bays, tidal channels, marshes, coastal prairie. Working harbor (fishing, shrimp boats, seafood, cargo), compact historic commercial center, older warehouses (some repurposed). Council–manager government (Mayor + 4 seats, Town Manager, Clerk, Public Works, Planning & Permits, Parks & Recreation). `established` [published] (`brackwaterAbout.html`, `brackwaterGovernment.html`)
- **Former industrial district:** a local designation referring to the area's industrial history, not to an absence of activity. It holds vacant older buildings alongside facilities that remain in operation or have been adapted for new uses (`brackwaterAbout.html`), including facilities receiving industrial water service (`brackwaterRecords.html`). `established` [published] Also where Salome held Room Study 03, in a disused building that "went up in the years when the town expected the harbor to keep growing", "It has been mostly empty for years" (`salomeData.js`, room-study-03). `established` [published]. **Connection:** Salome makes its own events inside infrastructure left over from Brackwater's unrealized expansion, the same expansion that enlarged Laguna Honda. The town's inherited, under-used buildings become the collective's temporary rooms. **[interpretation]** No street, building name or district name has been invented. **Employment history** [published]: ren's mom "used to pick up extra shifts in that building" and "couldn't believe people were going there voluntarily". The building was a workplace within living memory and has been mostly empty for years since; its original function is deliberately unspecified. `established` (use), `unresolved` (function).
  - **Tessera's Brackwater facility** occupies part of the district in existing buildings adapted for computing [published until 2026-11-19; still canon, no longer stated on `tessera.html`]. The district therefore holds an operating data center, vacant older buildings (e.g. Room Study 03's), and facilities served by industrial water. Which buildings Tessera uses, and whether any relation exists to Room Study 03's building, is `unresolved`. Not to be connected without direction.
- **Working harbor:** cranes, warehouses, docks. Site of festival installations. Some areas restricted to authorized personnel. Salome's 2026 commission projected onto harbor cranes and warehouse walls; a road-facing warehouse wall was removed from the plan at the Town's request [published].
- **Laguna Honda Reservoir:** inland natural freshwater pond enlarged in the early 20th century for anticipated growth that mostly did not come. One of "several freshwater resources." Surrounding land set aside as a wildlife park. Currently low water and rising salinity. `established`
- **Within the park:** visitor center (has a printer), north marsh trail, east observation platform (loose third board), western shoreline, south drainage channel (old concrete exposed), old intake (old fence posts visible), old pump house and the path behind it (**closed to the public**, per `lagunaHonda.html`), maintenance shed, office (folder of 30 years of measurements with gaps).
- **Wooden footbridge behind the old pump house:** remembered by a visitor and shown in a late-1980s brochure photo ("location unidentified"). The older keeper says it never existed and it is not on the maps. `unresolved`, deliberately.
- **Salome:** independent audiovisual collective based in Brackwater. `established`
- **Tessera Systems:** privately held technology company founded in 2020, developing proprietary AI software for border security and surveillance, defense intelligence and commercial AI (see B5). It operates the Brackwater facility; other facilities elsewhere are implied, unspecified. It did not originate in Brackwater. Headquarters, executives, named products, customers and contracts: **not established, not to be invented**. The Brackwater facility was approved during the Mayor's first term and **opened in 2024** [published, `tessera.html`, 2026-11-19; replaces "roughly one to two years, during his second term"]. The Town's "Now Operational" notice has been removed (`superseded`). The water agreement with the Town was executed September 12, 2023 (`provisional` date) and names Tessera. The Town calls the area simply "our industrial district" (About, the Mayor's statement), while Salome and the log keep "former industrial district". `established` [published + artist instruction]
- **Cruze:** search engine/browser framing the town. Whether it exists *inside* the fiction or acts as an outer frame is `unresolved`.
- **Regional geography:** the area was Mexican territory in the early 19th century, then "changes in territorial governance." Indigenous habitation predates the town. The Spanish place name and wildlife (roseate spoonbills, white pelicans, black-necked stilts, croaker, buttonbush) suggest a Gulf Coast setting resembling Texas, but no state or country is named. **[interpretation]** `unresolved`

## G. Themes and artistic contributions
*All items here are [interpretation] unless marked otherwise.*

- **Trans recognition:** the clearest instance is the Oct 30 log. The older keeper's well-meaning guess about what the younger keeper "hadn't gotten to do as a boy," the correction ("fixing things wasn't one of them"), "i told him he could ask" / "he said he didn't want to pry" / "i said that was fine too," and the wrench found in his own back pocket. Others: "she called me handsome… i've been thinking about it all afternoon. anyway, three spoonbills" (euphoria immediately deflected); learning to tie a line from a video at seventeen (self-taught skills where inherited teaching was missing); "the younger one came in… with a haircut. told him it looked fine. he said that wasn't much of a compliment."
- **The dominant myth of gender:** the "boyhood" exchange shows the binary as a default script about childhood. The older keeper can only imagine the younger keeper's past through it. The fishing kid's "did your dad teach you?" makes the same assumption in a lighter key. The municipal site is the binary's institutional parallel so far: complete-looking, authoritative, and empty of people.
- **The binary in ordinary institutions (2026-11-12):** [interpretation] ~~a municipal phone line's default "ma'am" (Nov 3 log)~~ (superseded 2026-11-15, removed from the log), and the Town's "Father & Son Fishing Day" at the same park where a boy asked the keeper "did your dad teach you?" (Oct 24) and where the keeper "hadn't gotten to do [things] as a boy" (Oct 30). Neither site comments. The connection appears only to someone who reads both.
- **Three perspectives on one development:** the Town ("employment and contracting opportunities"), Tessera (capability across domains; until 2026-11-19 "Water supply: Normal"), and the keepers (salinity "creeping up", calls to Public Works). The logs' own archive is "no longer available," the measurement folder has missing years, and the Town keeps "supporting materials… separately."
