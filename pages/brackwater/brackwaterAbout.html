About Brackwater

Brackwater is a small coastal town among shallow bays, tidal channels, and salt marsh. The water around us is mostly salt, which is why fresh water has always been one of the Town’s most important responsibilities.

Our History

Indigenous communities lived on this land long before the town was established. Brackwater was founded in the early nineteenth century, when the region was Mexican territory, and over the following decades its harbor became the center of local life.

In the early twentieth century, Brackwater prepared for greatness. The Town built new docks, warehouses, and industrial facilities for the regional port it expected to become. Laguna Honda, a natural freshwater pond inland from the harbor, was enlarged into a reservoir large enough to supply the many thousands of new residents that growth would bring.

Those residents have not yet arrived.

Brackwater Today

Brackwater kept what it built. Cranes still work the harbor, and warehouses still line the waterfront, some in use, others awaiting their next purpose.

The foresight of earlier generations continues to serve the town. Laguna Honda’s capacity was an important factor in bringing Tessera Systems to our industrial district, the kind of investment the reservoir was built for.

We are proud of where we come from, and confident in where we are going.
