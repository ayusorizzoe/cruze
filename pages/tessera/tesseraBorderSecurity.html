Border Security

Awareness beyond the line.

A border can be marked on a map, but the territory rarely follows it. Rivers run through its surface, coastlines change shape, and communities grow across divisions established long after their foundations were laid. What appears separate from a distance may have always been connected beneath the surface.

Tessera develops AI systems that bring information from multiple sensors into a shared operational picture, supporting detection, tracking, and human decision-making across terrestrial and maritime borders.

A changing landscape

The terrain does not stand still.

Boundaries may remain fixed while everything within them changes. Familiar contours soften, surfaces shift, and forms emerge that no longer resemble their original descriptions. A map may preserve an outline long after the territory has grown beyond it.

Tessera combines information from surveillance towers, ground sensors, aerial platforms, and maritime sources to maintain consistent awareness across changing environments.

Patterns of movement

Not every crossing begins at the border.

Some paths are inherited, worn into the ground by generations of passage. Others emerge where the terrain offers no recognized opening. A body may follow a route that feels entirely natural to it, even when the surrounding boundaries provide no place for its passage.

Tessera’s systems connect observations across locations and over time, helping operators identify patterns, track movement, and distinguish activity requiring further attention.

Human oversight

Technology informs. People decide.

A presence can be detected without being understood. A body can be observed in motion without revealing where it belongs. Even the most accurate observation cannot account for everything that brought someone to a particular place.

Tessera’s systems organize observations according to criteria established by security agencies. Human operators review the resulting assessments and determine whether further action is warranted. The technology supports these decisions without independently authorizing enforcement.
