Laguna Honda Wildlife Park

Brackwater, coastal wetlands and reservoir

About the park

History

Laguna Honda began as a natural freshwater pond inland from Brackwater. During the town’s early twentieth-century period of industrial expansion, the pond was enlarged into a reservoir to support the population growth the Town anticipated.

When that growth did not arrive, the surrounding wetlands became the focus of a new effort to promote Brackwater’s natural landscape. The Town established Laguna Honda Wildlife Park around the reservoir, setting aside an extensive area of shoreline and wetlands for conservation and public recreation.

Only part of the park was developed for visitor access, with trails, observation platforms, and other facilities concentrated around selected sections of the reservoir. Over the years, some of these facilities have closed or become restricted as maintenance and staffing needs have changed.

Laguna Honda Reservoir

The reservoir is one of several freshwater resources serving the Brackwater area. Water supply from the reservoir is managed by the Town of Brackwater (see water service information). The park looks after the shoreline, the surrounding wetlands, and public access.

Freshwater wetlands

The park’s wetlands include reed beds, shallow margins, drainage channels, and marsh vegetation such as buttonbush. These habitats shelter birds, fish, and other wildlife, and belong to the larger coastal landscape beyond the park.

Birds

Birds commonly seen at the park include roseate spoonbills, black-necked stilts, and, in the cooler months, white pelicans. Wading birds are easiest to find along the shallows. Recent sightings are noted in the keeper logs.

Trails and observation areas

Laguna Honda Wildlife Park covers an extensive area of reservoir shoreline and surrounding wetlands, although only designated sections are maintained for public access.

Visitor trails follow parts of the shoreline and the north marsh, with observation platforms overlooking the shallows. Other areas remain undeveloped or are closed because of maintenance and access restrictions.

The visitor center is the main point of contact for questions about trail conditions and accessible areas. See Visit for current visitor information and guidance.

Staff

The park is run by a small staff of keepers, who maintain the trails and platforms, watch the wildlife, and answer visitors’ questions. Their observations are recorded in the keeper logs.

Current environmental concerns

Water levels at the reservoir have been lower than usual in recent months, and staff have noted rising salinity in parts of the reservoir. Keepers record water levels each morning and report their observations to the Town. Water level and quality monitoring for the reservoir’s supply operations is carried out separately under the Town’s agreements. Recent observations are noted in the keeper logs.
